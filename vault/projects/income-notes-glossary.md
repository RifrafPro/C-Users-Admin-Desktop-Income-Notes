# Income Notes — Glossary of Key Terms

> Saved copy of the "Income Notes Glossary" Google Doc (shared with Rich).
> Source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1k_nZAjBaZ7i0feACXyzVDfCHU8aM6jtmTsvabtbQx70/edit

**Acceleration** — If the borrower defaults, the lender may accelerate the loan, making the entire outstanding balance due.

**Adjustable Rate Mortgage (ARM)** — A home loan whose interest rate adjusts over time with the market. Lower initial rate than fixed, but payments can fluctuate after the intro period.

**Allonge** — French, "to lengthen." For note purposes, a fancy word for "addendum."

**Amortization Schedule** — Table showing the loan's repayment plan; each periodic payment split between principal and interest over the life of the loan.

**Annualized Yield** — Annual rate of return on an investment, as a %. For notes: yearly income from the note ÷ initial investment.

**Balloon Payment** — Large lump-sum due at the end of a loan term, larger than the regular payments; common in commercial or short-term residential loans.

**Borrower** — The person borrowing the money.

**Broker's Price Opinion (BPO)** — Cost-effective property value estimate from a real estate pro; a cheaper alternative to a full appraisal for assessing note collateral.

**Collateral** — Asset/property pledged as security for a loan; the lender can pursue it if the borrower defaults.

**Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)** — Monthly debt payments vs. monthly gross income; used to gauge a borrower's ability to repay.

**Deed of Trust** — In some states, used instead of a mortgage; transfers title to a trustee who holds it as security for the loan.

**Default** — Any violation of the lending agreement's terms; the note defines what counts as default.

**Down Payment** — Portion of the purchase price paid up front; the rest is financed.

**Due on Sale Clause** — Requires the borrower to pay off the full balance if the property is sold/transferred; prevents loan assumption without lender approval.

**Escrow** — A third party holds/regulates funds for two parties; in real estate, often for taxes and insurance.

**FHA** — Federal Housing Administration; provides mortgage insurance on FHA-approved loans, with more lenient credit and lower down-payment options.

**Fixed Rate Mortgage (FRM)** — Interest rate stays constant for the loan's term.

**Foreclosure** — Legal process to recover a loan balance from a defaulted borrower by forcing the sale of the collateral.

**Graduated Payment Mortgage (GPM)** — Payments start low and rise over time; lets borrowers afford more up front, expecting income to grow.

**Hard Money Loan** — Short-term loan secured by real property, from private investors/companies; higher rates, faster approval.

**Interest Rate** — The amount of interest accruing each period.

**Investment to Value (ITV)** — Amount invested in a note ÷ value of the underlying property; note-investor analog to LTV.

**Junior Lien** — A lien subordinate to a more senior lien on the same property; paid after senior liens in foreclosure.

**Late Payment Fees** — Fees charged if the borrower pays late.

**Lender** — The person/entity issuing the money (private lender or bank).

**Letter of Intent (LOI)** — Non-binding doc expressing serious interest in buying a note/portfolio; outlines basic terms before full due diligence and a formal purchase agreement.

**LIBOR (London Interbank Offered Rate)** — Benchmark rate at which big banks lend to each other short-term; influenced many consumer loan rates. Being phased out.

**Lien Position** — Priority order in which lien holders are paid in foreclosure; first position is paid first.

**Loan to Value (LTV)** — Loan amount ÷ appraised property value, as a %. Higher LTV = riskier.

**Non-Performing Notes** — Notes where the borrower has stopped paying / is in default; often sold at a discount, higher risk/return.

**Note Modification** — A change to the original note terms (rate, payment, term), often to help a borrower avoid default.

**Par** — Selling "at par" = at the note's face value / original rate.

**Partial Note** — A portion of a note sold to an investor, who receives a proportional share of payments.

**Payment Due Date** — The date each payment must be made by.

**Payment Schedule** — Frequency and amount of required payments (weekly, monthly, etc.).

**Penalties** — Fines/fees if the borrower doesn't meet the note's obligations.

**Performing Notes** — Borrower is current on all payments. Good borrower + good collateral commands the highest price (near/at face value). Bought by income-seeking investors (institutions, funds, private investors).

**PITI** — Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance — the four parts of a typical mortgage payment.

**Prepayment Penalty** — Fee charged if the borrower pays off the loan early, usually a % of the balance.

**Principal** — Amount owed on the property after down payment, before interest/payments.

**Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)** — Protects the lender if the borrower stops paying; typically required on conventional loans with <20% down.

**Promissory Note** — Legal doc with the borrower's written promise to pay a stated sum at a set date or on demand.

**Rehabbing a Note** — Buy non-performing loans at a discount, modify terms with the borrower, then sell the re-performing note at a markup.

**REO (Real Estate Owned)** — Property acquired by a lender through foreclosure after the borrower defaulted and it didn't sell at auction.

**Seasoning** — How long a loan has existed and performed; seasoned loans are seen as less risky.

**Servicing** — Collecting payments and handling loan admin (payments, escrow, delinquencies).

**Short Sale** — Selling a property for less than the mortgage owed, with lender approval; an alternative to foreclosure.

**Subordination** — Ranking loans by priority; a subordinated loan has lower priority in a sale/foreclosure.

**Term of the Mortgage** — Details on payments, consequences of non-payment, and the time allowed to repay.

**Tranche** — A slice of a pooled set of securities split by risk/characteristics; in note investing, different risk/return layers.

**Underwriting** — Lender's process to decide if a loan's risk is acceptable (credit, employment, income, assets).

**Yield Spread** — Difference between a loan's rate and the rate at which it can be sold to investors; the lender/investor's potential profit.

**YSP (Yield Spread Premium)** — Compensation from a lender to a broker for selling a rate above the lender's minimum; a bonus for a higher borrower rate.
