## THE SCRIPT

**Opener**
> "Bruce? Rich Fabiani with FRESH GROUND — we're a land acquisition group here in Northern
> Virginia. We buy building lots and place them with custom builders. I'm calling about
> 2754 Chain Bridge in Vienna. Do you have five minutes?"

**⭐ Ask this EARLY — Script 02, step 2. It changes the whole call.**
> "Before we get into it — if this works out, would you represent me as the buyer? I'd rather
> you have both sides of it than me bring my own agent."

*Why: he earns both commissions, so he starts working for your offer instead of against it.
~80% say yes. This is also the answer to "isn't he just taking a cut" — the double-side is
exactly what buys you an advocate inside the deal.*

---

**Q1 — THE DEAL-DECIDER**
> "The listing describes two lots. Is that subdivision actually **recorded** — a recorded plat,
> two separate tax parcels — or is it two lots the R-2 zoning would permit, but a buyer still
> has to take through subdivision?"

*Listen for hedging. "It's zoned for two" ≠ "it's platted as two."*
Follow-up: **"Is there a recorded plat number I can pull?"**

**Q2 — THE ACREAGE (ask it, don't accuse)**
> "Help me reconcile something before I take this to my builder. The listing shows 1.492 acres.
> When I pull the parcel — PIN 0481-01-0017 — Fairfax has it at **1.066**, and the county GIS
> mapping agrees. Where does the 1.492 come from? Is there a second parcel I'm not seeing?"

*This is the single most powerful sentence in the call. It is factual, it is verifiable, and it
tells him you did work no other caller did. **Say it curiously, not as a gotcha** — you want him
as an ally, and an accusation makes him defensive on the sellers' behalf.*
Follow-up if he insists on 1.492: **"Which PIN is the second piece? I'll pull it."**

**Q3 — THE DEAD CONTRACT**
> "I see it went under contract in March last year and came back on. What happened there? I'd
> rather hear it from you now than find it myself in due diligence."

*Financing? Subdivision? Soils/perc? Title? Trust signature problem? Any of those is priceable —
but only if you know.*

**Q4 — THE FINANCING**
> "The listing mentions partial owner financing. What do the sellers have in mind — down payment,
> rate, term? And would they release one lot at a time, or is it all or nothing?"

*A seller offering financing wants a **structure** solved, not just a lower number. This is where
your leverage actually lives.*

**Q5 — THE PRICE (Script 02, step 10 — then shut up)**
> "What would it take to get this under contract this week?"

**→ SILENCE. Let him fill it.** Then:
> "How flexible are they, realistically?"
> "Would they review something less?"

*Do NOT name a number. You don't have a MAO yet.*

**Q6 — DEAL EXTRACTION (Script 02, step 14 — the real prize)**
> "Last thing. I noticed you've also got **10221 Oakton Drive** and **6252 23rd Street North**.
> I'm buying multiple lots this year. Do you have anything else in the pipeline — even off-market,
> something not listed yet — that you could represent me on?"

*He is sitting on three of the leads from our sweep. **This question is worth more than the
Chain Bridge deal itself.** It converts one call into a channel.*

> "No, I'm a principal. We buy lots and place them with builders we already work with. Sometimes

**"Do you have proof of funds?"**

**"There's other interest."**
