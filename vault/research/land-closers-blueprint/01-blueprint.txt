CHAPTER 1 Why Vacant Land? (And Why Almost Nobody Talks About It)  When most people think about real estate investing, they think about houses. Buy a fixer-upper, rehab it, sell it. Or buy a rental property, deal with tenants, collect checks. Both of those strategies work. They also come with contractors who ghost you, tenants who don't pay, surprise foundation issues, and enough stress to make you question why you got into this at all. Vacant land has none of that.  Land has no buildings to maintain. There's no roof to replace. No plumbing to fix. No tenant calling at 11pm about the heat. You own a piece of the earth. It just sits there. Land sellers are highly motivated. The people selling vacant land are almost never doing it because the land is appreciating. They're selling because they inherited it, forgot they owned it, are behind on taxes, or just want it gone. Many of them will accept 30-50 cents on the dollar just to be done with it. You can do this from your laptop. Every step of the Land Closers system, finding sellers, making offers, signing contracts, finding buyers, closing, happens remotely. You never need to visit the property. The entire business runs from your phone and computer. The margins are extraordinary. When you buy land at 30-50% of market value and sell it at or near market value, you're looking at 100-200%+ returns on your purchase price. On a deal where you buy at $18K and sell at $55K, that's a $37,000 profit. On one deal.  Land investors aren't smarter than everyone else. They just figured out that the boring asset everybody ignores is the one with the best risk-adjusted returns.

CHAPTER 3 Picking Your Market  Not all markets are equal. Send 18,000 texts in the wrong county and you'll get responses, but the deals won't be there. Send the same campaign in the right county and you'll be fielding offers.  The 5 Market Criteria  1. Population growth You want counties within 2 hours of a growing metro area. Where people are moving, land values appreciate and buyers are active. Sun Belt states, Texas, Florida, the Carolinas, Georgia, Tennessee, Arizona, consistently produce strong deal flow. 2. Median lot price in the $20K-$200K range Too cheap (under $5K) and your margins evaporate. Too expensive ($500K+) and you need more capital and longer timelines. The sweet spot is $20K-$200K, enough margin to make real money, liquid enough to sell quickly. 3. Active sales volume Look for 50-100+ vacant land sales in the county in the past 12 months. This confirms there's an active buyer market. No buyers means no exits, no matter how good your deal is. 4. Property taxes not too high High annual property taxes mean motivated sellers who are underwater on carrying costs. Avoid markets where taxes are so high that buyers balk at acquisition. 5. Not already saturated by land investors If you're seeing the same mailer from 5 different land investors every month, that county is too competitive. Use Land Portal or Redfin to check how many "lot" listings are sitting unsold. 90+ days on market is a red flag.  The best market for your first deal isn't the hottest market. It's the one with active buyers, motivated sellers, and enough deal volume that the math can work in your favor.

Your job is to find the ones who are most likely to sell, and then get their phone number.  Step 1: Pull Your List in Land Portal or Land Insights Land Portal and Land Insights are the tools we recommend for pulling targeted land seller lists. Both let you filter county property records down to exactly the sellers you want. For land investing, your basic filter looks like this:  • Property type: Vacant land / Lots • Owner type: Individual (not LLC or corporation) • Out-of-state owner (absentee): Yes, they're less emotionally attached • Years of ownership: 5+ years (longer = more motivated) • Tax delinquency: Yes (urgency to sell) • Lot size: Filter based on your target market  A typical filtered list for a single county run is 3,000-8,000 records. Export that as a CSV.  Step 2: Skip Trace Your List A property record gives you the owner's name and mailing address. Skip tracing appends a cell phone number so you can actually contact them. Our preferred tools:  • DirectSkip: Best overall quality for land owner data

CHAPTER 6 Making Offers: The Land Valuation System  This is where most beginners freeze. "How do I know what to offer?" The answer is a system. Once you know the system, every deal takes the same 15-20 minutes to evaluate.  Step 1: Find the Property If the seller gives you an address, use Zillow's zoom-in technique to pinpoint the exact parcel. If they only give you a parcel number, use the county's GIS map to locate it. Most counties have a free public GIS tool.

 Step 2: Pull Comparable Sales (The 3-Comp Rule) On Zillow, filter for: Lots/Land + Sold + Last 6 Months. You need at least 3 comparable sales to establish market value. Comparable means:  • Same general area (within 5-10 miles, same county) • Similar size (within 20-30% of your subject property) • MLS-verified sales only (look for the MLS logo + realtor names) • Exclude: bulk purchases, family transfers, foreclosure sales  Three solid comps give you a reliable market value. Average the three and that's your baseline.  Step 3: Adjust for Features Not all lots are equal. Here's how to adjust:  Value Increases Value Decreases Water access: +$50K-$100K+ Landlocked (no road access): -50% or worthless Ocean/lake view: +$20K-$50K Flood zone: -$10K-$30K Utilities on-site: +$10K-$20K Steep slope/wetlands: -$10K-$20K Paved road frontage: +$5K-$15K Unbuildable zoning: -80% or worthless  Step 4: Apply the Offer Formula Once you have adjusted market value, apply the scaled offer formula:  • Under $100K market value: Offer 50% of market value • $100K-$300K market value: Offer 50-60% of market value • $300K-$700K+ market value: Offer 70-75% of market value  Why does the percentage increase at higher values? Because a 50% offer on a $500K lot is a $250K lowball that kills the conversation. At higher values, you preserve dialogue while still protecting your spread.

The Free Market Intelligence Hack Before you make an offer, call the listing realtor on any recently sold comparable lot. Say: "Hi, I saw you sold a lot on [Street] for $X last month. I'm looking at buying one nearby. Was there anything special about that lot that drove the price?" Realtors love talking about their sales. In 5 minutes you'll know if the comp was an outlier, what features matter in that market, and whether prices are trending up or down. This is free intelligence that most investors never bother to get. CHAPTER 7 Contracts, Due Diligence, and Closing  You got a "yes" from a seller. Now what? This is where a lot of beginners panic. They don't know what contract to use, they don't know what to check, and they're afraid of making a mistake. The good news: this part is far more straightforward than it sounds.  Which Contract to Use  Purchase Agreement You're buying the property yourself and reselling it later. Best for deals where you have the capital to close and want to maximize your sale price and flexibility. Assignment Contract You lock up the deal under contract, then sell your contract rights to an end buyer before closing. You never actually own the land. This is the most capital-efficient approach and how most beginners start. Double Closing You close on the purchase and the sale in the same day, using the buyer's funds to purchase. Works when buyers won't accept an assignment structure.

 For your first deals: use an assignment contract. It requires the least capital and lets you move quickly.  The 60-Day Rule Always request at least 60 days in your contract with an automatic 30-day extension clause. You need time for due diligence, finding a buyer, and closing. Sellers often push back on the timeline. Hold firm. Anyone serious about selling will wait 60 days for a cash buyer.  Due Diligence: The 6 Essential Checks Due diligence is what separates professionals from people who get burned. Run these 6 checks on every deal before you commit:  • Zoning: Is the land buildable for the intended use? Call the county planning department. • Access: Is there road access? A landlocked lot is worth a fraction of its paper value. • Utilities: Water, sewer, electric, are they on-site, nearby, or nonexistent? • Environmental: Any known contamination, wetlands, or flood zone designations? • Title: Any liens, encumbrances, or ownership disputes on the title? • Perc Test: If the lot needs a septic system, can the soil support one?  Most of this information is publicly available. A title company will catch most title issues when you initiate closing. Your due diligence period (typically 2-4 weeks) gives you time to confirm the lot is what the seller says it is.  Working with Title Companies Title companies handle the closing. You don't need to be there in person. Ever. You send them the signed contract, they open escrow, do a title search, prepare closing documents, coordinate signatures via DocuSign, and wire funds. Your job is to email the title company: "Hi, I have a property under contract and I'm ready to open escrow." They take it from there.

CHAPTER 8 Finding Your Buyer and Getting Paid  This is the step most people worry about the least and should worry about the most. Having a great deal means nothing if you don't have a buyer. Here's the rule we operate by at Elevate: start building your buyer list before you have a deal.  The 3 Buyer Channels  Channel 1: Facebook Land Groups (Best for Speed) There are thousands of Facebook groups dedicated to buying and selling land. Search "[State] land for sale" or "vacant land investors [County]." Join the groups for every market you're targeting. When you have a deal, post: property description, photos (use Google Maps satellite view), comps showing market value, your asking price, and the deadline. Motivated buyers respond within hours. This is how beginners close their first deals in 24-72 hours.  Channel 2: Zillow Realtor Scraping (Best for Relationships) Filter Zillow for recently sold land in your target county. Find the realtors whose names appear most often on those sold listings. Call each one and say: "Hi, I'm a land investor in [County]. I buy lots at a discount and resell them. I'm looking for realtors who work with land buyers and would want first look at my deals. Is that something you'd be interested in?" Most will say yes. You've just built a referral relationship with someone who has an active buyer database. That's worth more than any Facebook post.  Channel 3: Builder Outreach (Best for Margins) Builders are the premium buyer channel. They pay cash, close fast, and buy repeatedly. A single builder relationship can generate $100,000+ in annual assignment fees.
