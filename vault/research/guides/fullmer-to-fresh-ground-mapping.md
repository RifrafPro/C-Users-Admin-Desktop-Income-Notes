| Fullmer's version | Our version | Why it changes |

| **Buy at 10–50¢ of as-is value** | **MAO from the land residual**, ceiling set by the builder's buy-box | His discount is a % of resale value. | **Four-Week Rule: 30 days unsold = overpriced** | **30 days with no builder bite = the price is wrong** | Same discipline, different audience. Applies to our assignment price, not an MLS listing |

## 🎯 THE ACTUAL SYNTHESIS — where Fullmer becomes our edge

Fullmer's thesis: *buy the problem nobody will touch.*
FRESH GROUND's thesis: *find teardown lots for luxury builders.*

**They intersect on exactly one thing: problem lots in expensive zips.**

On a clean, buildable McLean lot we compete with every builder and land buyer in NoVA, and margins
compress accordingly. On a lot with a **defect** — an RPA overlay, an unrecorded subdivision, heirs
on title, a lien stack, a landlocked parcel — the competition evaporates, and Fullmer's craft is
what lets us price and solve it. **That is the moat, and it is the only place his method makes us
money.** Two live examples already in the pipeline:

- **Old Chesterbrook Rd** — RPA exception + storm-drainage easements. Cheapest dirt in 22101 *because* of the problem.
- **2754 Chain Bridge Rd** — two lots that aren't recorded, held in a trust, with a contract that already died in diligence.

Both are Fullmer-shaped deals sitting in our market at 10× his price band.

**Operating rule going forward:** when a lot has a *legal* defect, run the Fullmer play (S4 recon →
option contract → solve or walk). When a lot is clean, run the normal FRESH GROUND play (S1 buy-box →
S5 MAO → S8 disposition). **Don't mix them.**
