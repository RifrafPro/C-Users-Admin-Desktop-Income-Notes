## The model in one paragraph
Buy properties nobody else CAN buy — because the value is trapped under title problems
(probate never filed, missing heirs, liens, judgments, delinquent taxes, 65-owner
inheritance messes). Pay 10–50¢ on the as-is dollar, spend a small legal budget
(~$10–26K/deal) fixing the PAPERWORK (never the house), then sell as-is on the MLS at
85–95¢ fast. "Paperwork, problems, and people — we're in the people business and trade
in real estate." Avg deal (his last 100): **$48K all-in → $194K sale → ~$125K net.**
Cycle time 90–120 days. No debt, no rehab, no ARV.

## Key principles (chapter by chapter)

### The math (Ch 7)
- **"ARV is a dirty word."** Only as-is value matters — what it sells for TOMORROW, trash
  in the yard, utilities off. ARV = construction, contractors, time, thin margins.
- **Velocity of money:** "a fast nickel beats a slow dime." Sell at 85–90¢ to move fast;
  3 × $40K in 12 months beats 1 × $30K in 6.
- **Buy at 30–50¢** (stretch: 10–50¢) of as-is value. The delta IS the safety margin —
  big enough that surprise liens, code violations, or a surfacing cousin can't kill the deal.
- Big margins = bulletproof in downturns: when 2023-24 rates spiked, he just lowered price
  and still profited; leveraged flippers went bankrupt.

### The lead source (Ch 8) — HIS #1 SECRET
- **County delinquent property tax list.** Public record, free or a few bucks from the tax
  assessor; his 3 lists cost <$1,000/yr and produced every biggest deal.
- **Filter: 2–3 YEARS delinquent** (year one = still in denial; year 2-3 = ready to talk).
  Also filter: older owners, long-term ownership, high delinquency, out-of-state owners.
- Unpaid taxes = the universal distress flare. Works for land, teardowns, warehouses.
- ~30 filtered leads → 1 deal ("we're snipers," vs hundreds of calls for PPC wholesalers).

### Seller conversations (Ch 9, 12, 19, 22)
- Opener (low & calm, uncertain, conversational): *"Hey, this is ___. I know this is out
  of the blue — I was calling about that lot on ___. I saw your name attached and wasn't
  sure if you still owned it or if I even have the right person. Mind if I ask a couple
  questions?"*
- The magic question: **"Why haven't y'all sold it before now?"** — unlocks the real story
  (no will, feuding siblings, code issues). The story reveals the path to the deal.
- Seller talks 70%+ of the call. Curiosity, not interrogation.
- **The honest-lowball frame:** *"I'm not going to be your highest offer — I might be your
  lowest. But I'm the guy who can actually close. If you want retail, I'm not your guy.
  If you want out of the mess and it handled, I'm the guy."* Plus full disclosure: *"I'm
  going to make good money if I get this done — and here's why: nobody else can buy
  messes like this."* Sellers RESPECT it; many call back days later.
- Diagnose their problem TO them before they understand it themselves ("sounds like it's
  still in your dad's name… that's why title is tied up; here's what it would take").
- Lower expectations, then exceed: "This won't be fast, might not even be possible, but
  I'll give an honest effort and keep you posted." Then actually deliver.
- People, not leads. Sometimes the job is groceries, a mover, or a lawyer referral.

### Paperwork (Ch 10)
- **Option contract FIRST** when anything is uncertain: $10–100 for the right (not
  obligation) to buy at an agreed price. 10–60-day option + 5–10-day close ≈ 90 days
  of control to run title and find owners; walk free if it's rotten. (Usually closes in
  2–3 days anyway — the time is insurance.)
- Then the **state promulgated purchase contract** (TREC in TX; VA/MD have equivalents) —
  courts, title cos, and realtors all recognize it. The inspection period = second option.
- **Earnest money: symbolic.** $100 has locked up million-dollar properties; ~1% looks
  best if a judge ever reads it.
- Assignments/JVs: fine, but DISCLOSE ("a partner is coming in; nothing changes for you;
  I stand to earn well if I solve this"). Undisclosed $80K fees blow up deals.

### Title forensics (Ch 11)
- Don't wait for the title company — run your own recon in 20 minutes: county appraisal
  district + county clerk: deed history, liens/abstracts (IRS, judgment, mechanics,
  child support, unreleased mortgages), open mortgages, probate/obituary clues.
- Third-party title reports exist online ~$200.
- Ask the seller the questions, but VERIFY everything — sellers are usually wrong, not lying.
- Red flags to slow down on: bankruptcies (trustee may own it), stacked liens, old private
  seller-finance notes, deceased owner w/ no probate, active lawsuits.
- Skills: read a title chain, use county records/docket/tax search, befriend an
  investor-savvy title officer.

### Desktop valuation (Ch 13)
- Two free sites: **County Appraisal District + Redfin/Zillow SOLD comps** (closed only).
- Average the comps, chop some off, be pessimistic by design. CAD $170K + comps
  $220/$185/$190K → call it $170K.
- Then offer 10–50¢ of that. No appraiser, no site visit needed at these margins.

### Solving the mess (Ch 14)
- "You don't need a toolbox of tools, you need a **toolbox of people**": investor-friendly
  title company, probate attorney, litigator, private investigator (his is retired FBI),
  genealogist for heir hunts.
- Curative work is the BUYER's value-add (title cos only escrow + insure).
- Ownership disputes = 70% of the game. 65-owner deal: bought $250K total ($170K sellers +
  $70K taxes + $10K legal) → worth $1.25M. **Work feuding heirs in SILOS — separate
  confidential contracts each; never force a family reunion.** "Don't punish me for your
  family's bad deeds — I'm the new guy with the white flag."
- **Defer payoffs to the closing table:** back taxes/liens can be paid from the END
  buyer's funds at disposition — his $10K-in / $140K-profit deal worked this way.

### Exit (Ch 15, 20)
- **Sell everything on the MLS via a realtor who understands as-is.** No wholesaling, no
  buyer's list, no assignments on the exit side — brokers bring vetted retail-investor
  buyers and the open market pays best.
- Prep = trash-out, mow, board windows, good photos. Never remodel.

### Tools (Ch 20)
- Lists: county tax assessor (near-free). Skip tracing: TLOxp (gold standard, PI access),
  Skip Genie/Smart Skip (starter), IDI Core/IRBsearch (scale). If skip trace fails:
  relatives, neighbors, Google, Facebook, obituaries — "you're a PI who happens to do
  real estate"; persistence past where others quit IS the edge.
- CRM: notebook/Sheets until a few dozen deals; then Trello/ClickUp/Airtable/Podio or
  REI-specific (he name-checks **REsimpli** — matches our pick); Salesforce at his scale.
- Batch Leads for data + lead management.

### No money (Ch 21)
- "**Good deals find money.**" Get the deal under contract first (option = pennies);
  capital partners split profits on real spreads. Being broke is a test, not a barrier.

## What NOT to adopt

- His model buys ANY distressed asset at huge discounts; ours is buyer-first teardown
