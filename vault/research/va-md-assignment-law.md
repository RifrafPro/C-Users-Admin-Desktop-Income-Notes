# 🔴 VIRGINIA — THE BIG ONE

## The statute, verbatim
**Va. Code § 54.1-2100, "Definitions"** — retrieved from `law.lis.virginia.gov`, page dated **9/3/2026**:

> **"Real estate broker"** means any individual or business entity, including a partnership,
> association, corporation, or limited liability company, who, **for compensation or valuable
> consideration**, (i) sells or offers for sale, buys or offers to buy, or negotiates the purchase
> or sale or exchange of real estate…; (ii) leases or offers to lease…; or **(iii) sells or offers
> to sell, buys or offers to buy, negotiates, or otherwise deals in real estate contracts,
> including assignable contracts, on two or more occasions in any 12-month period.**

Statutory history confirms the 2024 amendments: **2024, cc. 327, 352, 459, 621** (HB 917 and
companions, **effective July 1, 2024**).

## What this means, in plain terms
| | |
|---|---|
| **1 assignment** per rolling 12 months, for compensation | No license implicated by clause (iii) |
| **2 or more** assignments in any rolling 12-month period, for compensation | **You meet the statutory definition of a real estate broker** |

**The window is rolling** — measured from your last assignment, not from January 1.

## 🚨 Why this is existential for FRESH GROUND as currently designed
Our stated model is **repeated assignment of teardown-lot contracts to builders for a $30–75K fee.**
On a plain reading of clause (iii), **the second such assignment in any 12-month period makes us a
"real estate broker" under Virginia law** — which requires licensure.

Three details make it worse rather than better:
1. **It is not limited to residential.** Clause (iii) says "real estate contracts" generally — so
   our **vacant land** contracts in McLean, Vienna, Great Falls and Oakton are squarely inside it.
   (Contrast Maryland below, which is residential-only.)
2. **"Offers to sell" and "negotiates" are in the trigger.** It is not only completed assignments —
   the text reaches *offering* and *negotiating*. Our S8 buyer blast may itself be an "occasion."
   **This needs counsel; do not assume only closed deals count.**
3. **The threshold is two, not several.** There is no meaningful volume allowance.

## Possible compliance paths — for counsel to confirm, not for us to pick alone
| Path | What it is | Honest assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Take title and resell** ⭐ | Actually buy the lot (or double close), then sell property you own rather than dealing in a contract | **Most likely clean**, because clause (iii) targets dealing *in contracts*. **This is precisely the Fullmer technique we had parked** — see `fullmer-to-fresh-ground-mapping.md`. It needs capital or a funding partner, which changes the model but preserves it |
| **B. Get licensed** | Become a licensed broker, or affiliate | VA broker licensure is a high bar — generally requires prior active salesperson experience plus education. Slow, but permanent |
| **C. Work under a principal broker** | Partner with an existing licensed broker who handles transactions | Faster than B. Costs a split. Worth pricing |
| **D. One assignment per 12 months** | Stay under the threshold | Not a business |
| **E. Builder buys direct, we take a fee** | Never contract ourselves; introduce and get paid | ⚠️ **Probably the worst option** — an introduction fee for compensation may look even more like unlicensed brokerage under clause (i). Do not assume this is a loophole |

**Working recommendation until counsel says otherwise: assume path A.** It also happens to be the
higher-margin model Rich has said he wants at maturity, and it makes the parked Fullmer material
(take title → clear title → resell) immediately relevant rather than theoretical.

**Regulator:** Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR), Richmond —
Real Estate Board.

# 🟡 MARYLAND — disclosure regime, not a licensing bar

## The statute, verbatim
**Md. Real Property § 10-715**, added by **HB 124 / Chapter 508 (2025)**, signed **May 13, 2025**,
**effective October 1, 2025** — retrieved from the Maryland General Assembly's own chapter text:

**Definitions (A):**
- **"Residential property"** = property improved by **four or fewer single-family dwelling units**
  designed principally and intended for human habitation.
- **"Wholesale buyer"** = a person that enters into a contract of sale with an owner of
  **owner-occupied residential property** in order to assign the contract to another person **for a
  monetary assignment fee**.
- **"Wholesale seller"** = a person that, **without holding legal title** to owner-occupied
  residential property, (i) enters into a contract of sale, and (ii) assigns their beneficial
  interest to another.

**The two disclosures (C):**
1. **(C)(1)** — **BEFORE entering into the contract of sale**, the wholesale buyer **shall disclose
   in writing to the owner** that the wholesale buyer **may assign the contract of sale.**
2. **(C)(2)** — **BEFORE ASSIGNING** the contract, the wholesale seller **shall disclose in writing
   to the prospective assignee** that they **hold an equitable interest** and **may not be able to
   convey title.**

**The teeth (D) and (E):**
- **(D)** The owner may **rescind the contract without penalty at any time before closing** if the
  wholesale buyer did not give notice **and** assigns the contract.
- **(E)** The assignee may **rescind without penalty before closing** if no notice was given, and
  **is entitled to a refund of any deposit** paid in connection with the assignment.

**Carve-out (B):** does not apply to a sale proceeding under **Md. Rule 14-215** (foreclosure sales).
**Prospective only** — no effect on contracts executed before Oct 1, 2025.
Also cross-referenced into **§ 14-117(e)(24)** as a required contract-of-sale provision.

## What this means for us
- **Maryland does not require a license to assign** — you are assigning your own equitable interest,
  not acting "for another person." The bar is **disclosure**, not licensure.
- ⚠️ **Both disclosures are mandatory and timed.** Missing either hands the other side a
  **free rescission right up to closing** — i.e. your deal can evaporate the day before settlement.
- 🔍 **Scope question worth confirming with counsel:** § 10-715 reaches **residential property**
  (≤4 dwelling units) and, in both wholesale definitions, **owner-occupied** residential property.
  A **vacant lot** is not "improved by … dwelling units," so **our MD land deals may fall outside
  it — but our Bethesda/Potomac teardown-house deals are inside it.** Do not guess; confirm.

**Regulator:** Maryland Real Estate Commission, Department of Labor.
